faqs:
  - question: "How do pets get heartworms?"
    answer: |-
      <p>Heartworms are spread by mosquitoes. When a mosquito bites an infected animal, it picks up tiny baby worms, which develop inside the mosquito. When that mosquito bites another dog or cat, the larvae enter through the bite wound. It takes about six months for them to grow into adult heartworms.</p><p class="faq-source">Source: <a href="https://www.heartwormsociety.org/pet-owner-resources/heartworm-basics">American Heartworm Society, Heartworm Basics</a></p>
  - question: "Why does my dog need a heartworm test every year if they take a preventive?"
    answer: |-
      <p>Heartworm preventives are highly effective, but not 100 percent effective. Missing a single dose or giving it late can leave your dog unprotected, and a dog may spit out or vomit a pill or rub off a topical medication. The American Heartworm Society recommends testing every dog every year to make sure the prevention program is working.</p><p class="faq-source">Source: <a href="https://www.heartwormsociety.org/pet-owner-resources/heartworm-basics">American Heartworm Society, Heartworm Basics</a></p>
  - question: "What are the signs of heartworm disease in dogs?"
    answer: |-
      <p>Early stages of infection often cause no obvious signs. As the disease progresses, dogs may develop a soft, dry cough, shortness of breath especially after exercise, weakness and loss of stamina, or fainting. By the time signs are noticed, the disease is usually well advanced — which is why regular testing matters.</p><p class="faq-source">Source: <a href="https://vcahospitals.com/know-your-pet/heartworm-disease-in-dogs">VCA Animal Hospitals, Heartworm Disease in Dogs</a></p>
  - question: "What happens if my dog tests positive for heartworms?"
    answer: |-
      <p>Most infected dogs can be successfully treated. A positive result is first confirmed with a different test, and your dog may need to be stabilized before treatment begins. After treatment, a follow-up heartworm test confirms the worms have been eliminated, and year-round prevention helps keep your dog from being infected again.</p><p class="faq-source">Source: <a href="https://www.heartwormsociety.org/pet-owner-resources/heartworm-basics">American Heartworm Society, Heartworm Basics</a></p>
  - question: "Can cats get heartworm disease?"
    answer: |-
      <p>Yes. Heartworm disease in cats is different from the disease in dogs — cats usually have only a few worms, and it can be harder to detect — but even immature worms can damage a cat’s lungs. The medication used to treat heartworms in dogs can’t be used in cats, so prevention is the only way to protect them, and indoor cats are at risk too.</p><p class="faq-source">Source: <a href="https://www.heartwormsociety.org/pet-owner-resources/heartworm-basics">American Heartworm Society, Heartworm Basics</a></p>
