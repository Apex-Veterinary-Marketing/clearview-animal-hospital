  - question: "How do I know if my pet’s problem is an emergency?"
    answer: |-
      <p>Trouble breathing, collapse or unconsciousness, heavy bleeding, seizures that last more than a few minutes or keep coming, repeated vomiting or diarrhea (especially with blood), a swollen belly, suspected poisoning, eye injuries, heatstroke and any injury from a car accident all need immediate care. Even if your pet seems fine after an accident, it should still be examined.</p><p class="faq-source">Source: <a href="https://vcahospitals.com/know-your-pet/emergencies-in-dogs">VCA Animal Hospitals, Emergencies in Dogs</a></p>

    answer: |-
      <p>Call your veterinarian or an animal poison control center right away, and bring the product or its packaging with you. Don’t try to make your pet vomit unless a veterinarian tells you to. The ASPCA Animal Poison Control Center is available 24 hours a day at (888) 426-4435; a consultation fee may apply.</p><p class="faq-source">Source: <a href="https://www.aspca.org/pet-care/animal-poison-control">ASPCA, Animal Poison Control</a></p>

    answer: |-
