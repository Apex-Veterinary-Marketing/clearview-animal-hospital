      <h2>Skin &amp; Ear Infection Care Near Easley, SC</h2><p>Constant scratching, head shaking, a bad smell from the ears, red bumps or patches of missing hair — skin and ear problems are some of the most common reasons pets visit the veterinarian, and they can make your dog or cat truly miserable. The good news is that most of them can be treated once we know what’s behind them.</p><p>Skin and ear infections are usually caused by bacteria, yeast or parasites such as ear mites, and they often start because of something else, like allergies, fleas or a hormonal condition. At Clearview Animal Hospital, testing for skin and ear infections is one of the services handled through our <a href="/services/laboratory">in-house laboratory</a>. Looking at a sample under the microscope shows us what kind of infection your pet has, so we can choose the right medication instead of guessing.</p>

      <h2>What to Expect at Your Pet’s Visit</h2><p>We’ll start by asking about your pet’s symptoms — how long they’ve been going on, whether they come and go, and what your pet eats and is treated with for fleas. Then we’ll examine the skin and coat and look deep into the ears with an otoscope, a lighted scope that lets us see the ear canal and check whether the eardrum is intact.</p><p>Next, we usually collect a sample. For ears, that means a gentle swab of the debris in the canal. For skin, it may be a swab, a piece of clear tape pressed against the skin, or a light scraping. These samples are stained and examined under the microscope through <a href="/services/cytology">cytology</a>, which shows bacteria, yeast, mites and inflammatory cells. If an ear is very painful or packed with debris, your pet may need sedation so the ear can be examined and cleaned comfortably. For infections that keep coming back, we may recommend additional tests, such as a culture.</p><p>Treatment depends on what we find and may include ear medications, medicated shampoos or sprays, or medications by mouth. We’ll show you how to clean and medicate your pet’s ears at home and let you know when to come back for a recheck.</p>

      <h2>Does Your Pet Have a Skin or Ear Infection?</h2><p>Schedule an exam if your pet is shaking their head or scratching at their ears, has red or swollen ears, ear discharge or an unpleasant odor, is licking, chewing or scratching their skin, or has red bumps, crusts, flaky patches or hair loss. Dogs with large, floppy or hairy ears and pets with allergies are especially prone to these problems, but any dog or cat can be affected.</p><h2>How Often Should Your Pet Be Checked?</h2><p>Have your pet seen whenever signs of a skin or ear problem appear — the sooner an infection is treated, the easier it usually is to clear. Once treatment starts, rechecks help confirm the infection is completely gone before medication is stopped. Pets with allergies or infections that keep coming back may need regular visits so we can manage the underlying cause and catch flare-ups early.</p><h2>Practice Highlights</h2><p>Why families choose Clearview for itchy skin and ear trouble:</p><ul><li><strong>In-house laboratory</strong> — Skin and ear samples can be examined right here at our hospital.</li><li><strong>Caring for Easley since 1958</strong> — A trusted local animal hospital for generations of Upstate pets.</li><li><strong>Payment options</strong> — CareCredit and Cherry can help you plan for the cost of care.</li></ul>

faqs:
  - question: "What causes ear infections in dogs?"
    answer: |-
      <p>Ear infections are commonly caused by bacteria and yeast. Ear mites, foreign material, polyps and tumors can also be involved, and allergies or hormonal problems such as hypothyroidism are frequent underlying causes. Dogs with large, floppy or hairy ears are more prone, but any breed can be affected.</p><p class="faq-source">Source: <a href="https://vcahospitals.com/know-your-pet/ear-infections-in-dogs">VCA Animal Hospitals, Ear Infections in Dogs (Otitis Externa)</a></p>
  - question: "Why does my pet keep getting ear infections?"
    answer: |-
      <p>Repeated ear infections are often linked to an underlying problem, such as allergies or hypothyroidism. Medication can clear the current infection, but unless the underlying disease is diagnosed and treated, the ear problems are likely to continue. Several recheck exams may be needed before treatment is successful.</p><p class="faq-source">Source: <a href="https://vcahospitals.com/know-your-pet/ear-infections-in-dogs">VCA Animal Hospitals, Ear Infections in Dogs (Otitis Externa)</a></p>
  - question: "Can I clean my dog’s infected ears with cotton swabs or a home remedy?"
    answer: |-
      <p>No. Cotton swabs can push debris deeper into the ear canal, and home remedies such as vinegar mixtures can irritate inflamed ears and make swelling and discharge worse. Use the cleaner your veterinarian recommends, and keep treating until the infection is completely gone — dogs with bacterial or yeast infections usually need repeat checkups until no infection is seen.</p><p class="faq-source">Source: <a href="https://www.merckvetmanual.com/dog-owners/ear-disorders-of-dogs/ear-infections-and-otitis-externa-in-dogs">Merck Veterinary Manual, Ear Infections and Otitis Externa in Dogs</a></p>
  - question: "Do cats get ear infections too?"
    answer: |-
      <p>Yes, although bacterial and yeast ear infections are more common in dogs than cats. In cats, ear mites are the most common cause, especially in kittens and outdoor cats. Over-the-counter drops shouldn’t be used without a diagnosis, because some medications can harm the ear if the eardrum is damaged. Most diagnosed and treated infections can be cured.</p><p class="faq-source">Source: <a href="https://vcahospitals.com/know-your-pet/ear-infections-in-cats">VCA Animal Hospitals, Ear Infections in Cats (Otitis Externa)</a></p>
  - question: "What is a bacterial skin infection, and how long does treatment take?"
    answer: |-
      <p>A bacterial skin infection, called pyoderma, can cause red bumps with pus-filled centers, crusts, flaky patches, hair loss and itching. It is often secondary to allergies, because scratching damages the skin. Antibiotics are typically given for at least three to four weeks, and chronic or recurring cases may need longer treatment and testing for an underlying condition.</p><p class="faq-source">Source: <a href="https://vcahospitals.com/know-your-pet/pyoderma-in-dogs">VCA Animal Hospitals, Pyoderma in Dogs</a></p>
