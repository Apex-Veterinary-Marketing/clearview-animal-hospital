The good news is that most of them can be treated once we know what’s behind them.</p><p>Skin and ear infections are usually caused by bacteria, yeast or parasites such as ear mites, and they often start because of something else, like allergies, fleas or a hormonal condition.       <h2>What to Expect During Your Pet’s Skin &amp; Ear Visit</h2><p>Here’s how we get to the bottom of your pet’s itching, odor or discharge.</p><h3>History and Exam</h3><p>We’ll start by asking about your pet’s symptoms: how long they’ve been going on, whether they come and go, and what your pet eats and is treated with for fleas. Then we’ll examine the skin and coat and look deep into the ears with an otoscope, a lighted scope that lets us see the ear canal and check whether the eardrum is intact.</p><h3>Collecting a Sample</h3><p>For ears, that means a gentle swab of the debris in the canal. For skin, it may be a swab, a piece of clear tape pressed against the skin, or a light scraping. If an ear is very painful or packed with debris, your pet may need sedation so the ear can be examined and cleaned comfortably.</p><h3>Looking Under the Microscope</h3><p>These samples are stained and examined under the microscope through <a href="/services/cytology">cytology</a>, which shows bacteria, yeast, mites and inflammatory cells. For infections that keep coming back, we may recommend additional tests, such as a culture.</p><h3>Treatment and Home Care</h3><p>Treatment depends on what we find and may include ear medications, medicated shampoos or sprays, or medications by mouth. We’ll show you how to clean and medicate your pet’s ears at home and let you know when to come back for a recheck.</p>

Once treatment starts, rechecks help confirm the infection is completely gone before medication is stopped.     answer: |-
      <p>Testing can identify bacterial and yeast infections, ear mites and other parasites, and inflammation. A bacterial skin infection, called pyoderma, can cause red bumps, crusts, flaky patches, hair loss and itching, and it is often secondary to allergies. Repeated infections can point to an underlying condition that needs its own treatment, such as allergies or hypothyroidism.</p>
