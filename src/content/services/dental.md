      <h2>What to Expect During Your Pet’s Dental Cleaning</h2><p>A professional dental cleaning is done under general anesthesia. That keeps your pet still, comfortable and safe, and it lets us clean and examine every tooth — including below the gumline, where most dental disease hides.</p><p>The cleaning itself includes ultrasonic scaling to remove plaque and tartar from the tooth surfaces and under the gums, followed by polishing to smooth the enamel so plaque has a harder time sticking. faqs:
  - question: "Why does my pet need anesthesia for a dental cleaning?"
    answer: |-
      <p>A full oral exam, dental X-rays and cleaning under the gumline aren’t possible on an awake pet. Scraping tartar off an awake pet’s teeth is cosmetic only — it doesn’t diagnose or treat the disease below the gums. General anesthesia lets the veterinary team do a complete, safe and thorough job.</p><p class="faq-source">Source: <a href="https://vcahospitals.com/know-your-pet/dental-disease-in-cats">VCA Animal Hospitals, Dental Disease in Cats</a></p>
  - question: "How common is dental disease in pets?"
    answer: |-
      <p>Very common. Periodontal disease is the most common dental condition in dogs and cats, and by the time a pet is 3 years old, it will very likely have some early signs of it. Without preventive care, it gets worse as pets age.</p><p class="faq-source">Source: <a href="https://www.avma.org/resources-tools/pet-owners/petcare/pet-dental-care">American Veterinary Medical Association, Pet dental care</a></p>
  - question: "Why are dental X-rays part of the cleaning?"
    answer: |-
      <p>Much of each tooth sits below the gumline, out of sight. Dental X-rays let the veterinary team see the roots and surrounding bone and find problems — like infection, bone loss or damaged roots — that a visual exam would miss.</p><p class="faq-source">Source: <a href="https://vcahospitals.com/know-your-pet/dental-disease-in-cats">VCA Animal Hospitals, Dental Disease in Cats</a></p>
  - question: "How can I care for my pet’s teeth at home?"
    answer: |-
      <p>Daily brushing is the gold standard. Use a toothpaste made for pets — never human toothpaste — and go slowly so your pet learns to enjoy it. Dental treats, chews, diets and water additives that carry the Veterinary Oral Health Council (VOHC) seal can help too. Avoid scraping tartar at home with metal tools.</p><p class="faq-source">Source: <a href="https://vcahospitals.com/know-your-pet/dental-disease-in-cats">VCA Animal Hospitals, Dental Disease in Cats</a></p>
  - question: "Is my pet checked before going under anesthesia?"
    answer: |-
      <p>Pre-anesthetic bloodwork is usually recommended to check how your pet’s organs, such as the liver and kidneys, are working and whether your pet is a good candidate for anesthesia. If something abnormal shows up, the plan can be adjusted to keep your pet safe.</p><p class="faq-source">Source: <a href="https://vcahospitals.com/know-your-pet/bloodwork">VCA Animal Hospitals, Preanesthetic Bloodwork</a></p>
