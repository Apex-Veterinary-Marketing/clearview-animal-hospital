faqs:
  - question: "What are the health benefits of spaying my pet?"
    answer: |-
      <p>Spaying prevents uterine infections and decreases the risk of breast tumors, which are malignant in about 50 percent of dogs and 90 percent of cats. Spaying before the first heat offers the best protection. Spayed females also won’t go into heat.</p><p class="faq-source">Source: <a href="https://www.aspca.org/pet-care/general-pet-care/spayneuter-your-pet">ASPCA, Spay/Neuter Your Pet</a></p>
  - question: "What are the benefits of neutering my dog?"
    answer: |-
      <p>Neutering eliminates the risk of testicular cancer and reduces the risk of an enlarged or infected prostate. It also reduces hormonal behaviors such as mounting and marking, and makes roaming less likely, which can help prevent traffic accidents and other injuries.</p><p class="faq-source">Source: <a href="https://vcahospitals.com/know-your-pet/neutering-in-dogs">VCA Animal Hospitals, Neutering in Dogs</a></p>
  - question: "Will spaying or neutering make my pet overweight?"
    answer: |-
      <p>Spaying or neutering will not cause your pet to become overweight — overfeeding and lack of exercise do. Your pet will stay fit and trim if you keep providing exercise and keep an eye on how much they eat.</p><p class="faq-source">Source: <a href="https://www.aspca.org/pet-care/general-pet-care/spayneuter-your-pet">ASPCA, Spay/Neuter Your Pet</a></p>
  - question: "When is the best time to spay my dog?"
    answer: |-
      <p>Many factors go into the decision, including your dog’s health, behavior and environment. Spaying before the first heat protects against breast cancer, decreases the risk of bleeding after surgery and allows a faster recovery, while large-breed dogs may benefit from being spayed at an older age. Your veterinarian can help you choose the best time.</p><p class="faq-source">Source: <a href="https://vcahospitals.com/know-your-pet/spaying-in-dogs">VCA Animal Hospitals, Spaying in Dogs</a></p>
  - question: "How long does recovery take after a spay or neuter?"
    answer: |-
      <p>Most dogs can return to normal activity 5 to 10 days after surgery. Until then, stick to leash walks and plenty of rest, with no running, swimming or bathing, and use an E-collar or similar device so your pet can’t lick the incision.</p><p class="faq-source">Source: <a href="https://vcahospitals.com/know-your-pet/spaying-in-dogs">VCA Animal Hospitals, Spaying in Dogs</a></p>
