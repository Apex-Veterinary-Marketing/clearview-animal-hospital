faqs:
  - question: "Is every lump on my pet cancer?"
    answer: |-
      <p>No. Tumors are abnormal growths of cells, and many are benign — they don’t invade nearby tissue or spread and are usually easy to remove. Others are malignant and can spread. Telling them apart takes laboratory testing, so your veterinarian may collect cells with a fine needle or take a biopsy for evaluation.</p><p class="faq-source">Source: <a href="https://www.merckvetmanual.com/dog-owners/skin-disorders-of-dogs/tumors-of-the-skin-in-dogs">Merck Veterinary Manual, Tumors of the Skin in Dogs</a></p>
  - question: "Which lumps are most concerning?"
    answer: |-
      <p>Veterinarians are particularly concerned about lumps or masses that appear suddenly or grow rapidly, since these are traits associated with malignant skin tumors. Any suspicious skin lesion, or skin that is poorly healing, chronically inflamed or unusual in color or texture, should be checked and may need a biopsy.</p><p class="faq-source">Source: <a href="https://vcahospitals.com/know-your-pet/biopsy">VCA Animal Hospitals, Skin Biopsy in Dogs</a></p>
  - question: "Why is extra tissue removed around a tumor?"
    answer: |-
      <p>For most tumors, surgical removal is the most effective treatment. If a growth may be malignant, tissue around it is removed as well to increase the chance that no tumor cells are left behind. Afterward, the tumor is examined under a microscope (histopathology) to confirm its type and whether all of it was likely removed.</p><p class="faq-source">Source: <a href="https://www.merckvetmanual.com/dog-owners/skin-disorders-of-dogs/tumors-of-the-skin-in-dogs">Merck Veterinary Manual, Tumors of the Skin in Dogs</a></p>
  - question: "What is a mast cell tumor?"
    answer: |-
      <p>Mast cell tumors are a common type of skin tumor in dogs. In almost all cases they can be diagnosed with a simple needle sample of the tumor. Their grade, which guides the outlook for your pet, can only be determined after the tumor is surgically removed and examined by histopathology.</p><p class="faq-source">Source: <a href="https://www.acvs.org/small-animal/mast-cell-tumors">American College of Veterinary Surgeons, Mast Cell Tumors</a></p>
  - question: "How do I care for my pet after a lump is removed?"
    answer: |-
      <p>Keep your pet quiet, with no running, jumping or rough play for the time your veterinarian recommends, and use a cone or recovery suit so they can’t lick or chew the stitches. Check the incision every day: mild redness and swelling should slowly improve, but call your veterinary team if you see more than mild swelling, any discharge or open stitches.</p><p class="faq-source">Source: <a href="https://vcahospitals.com/know-your-pet/surgical-discharge-instructions-for-dogs">VCA Animal Hospitals, Surgical Discharge Instructions for Dogs</a></p>
