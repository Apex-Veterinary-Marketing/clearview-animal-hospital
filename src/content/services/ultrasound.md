      <h2>What to Expect During Your Pet’s Ultrasound</h2><p>An ultrasound exam is painless and non-invasive. Here’s what happens during the visit.</p><h3>Getting Ready</h3><p>We may ask you not to feed your pet beforehand, since a full stomach can make some organs harder to see. Most pets don’t need sedation, although a mild sedative can help a pet who is very nervous or wiggly.</p><h3>Comfortable Positioning</h3><p>Your pet lies comfortably on a padded table, usually on their back or side, while a team member gently helps them stay still.</p><h3>The Scan</h3><p>Because sound waves don’t travel well through air, a small patch of fur over the area being examined is usually shaved, and a water-based gel is applied to the skin. We then move a small handheld probe over the area and watch the images on screen as we go.</p><h3>Reviewing the Findings</h3><p>Because the images appear live, we can often share initial findings with you right away. We’ll explain what we saw, how it fits with your pet’s other test results, and what we recommend next.</p>

    answer: |-
      <p>Ultrasound uses sound waves to create a live, moving picture of the soft tissues inside your pet’s body. It’s recommended when there’s a specific question to answer, such as what’s causing ongoing vomiting, urinary problems, abnormal bloodwork or a swollen belly. Ultrasound uses no radiation, but air and bone block the sound waves, so it isn’t useful for normal lungs, bones, the brain or the spinal cord, which is why it’s often paired with X-rays.</p>
