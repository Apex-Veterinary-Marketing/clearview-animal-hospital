      <h2>FIV and FeLV Testing for Cats in Easley, SC</h2><p>Welcome to Clearview Animal Hospital, where our veterinary team helps cat owners in Easley, Pickens, Clemson, Liberty and Greenville know their cat’s FIV and FeLV status.</p><h2>Understanding FIV and FeLV Testing</h2><p>Feline immunodeficiency virus (FIV) and feline leukemia virus (FeLV) are two of the most common serious infections in cats. Both can weaken a cat’s immune system, and FeLV is also linked to cancer and blood disorders. The tricky part is that infected cats often look perfectly healthy for months or even years, so testing is the only way to know your cat’s status.</p><p>At Clearview Animal Hospital, FIV/FeLV testing is one of the tests handled through our <a href="/services/laboratory">in-house laboratory</a> services. Knowing whether your cat carries either virus helps you protect the other cats in your home, helps us interpret symptoms if your cat gets sick, and lets us tailor your cat’s preventive care to the risks they actually face.</p>

      <h2>What to Expect During Your Cat’s FIV/FeLV Test</h2><p>FIV/FeLV testing is a simple blood test. Here’s what the visit looks like.</p><h3>Learning Your Cat’s History</h3><p>We’ll ask about your cat’s history: where they came from, whether they go outdoors, whether they live with other cats, and whether they’ve had any fights or bite wounds. That background helps us read the results correctly.</p><h3>A Small Blood Sample</h3><p>The test starts with a small blood sample, which most cats handle well.</p><h3>Interpreting the Results</h3><p>A negative result is reassuring, but it may not be the final word if your cat was exposed very recently, since it can take time for the infection to show up on a test. A positive result in a cat that seems healthy is usually confirmed with follow-up testing before any conclusions are drawn. Kittens can also test positive for FIV because of antibodies passed on from their mother, so young kittens may need to be retested as they grow.</p><h3>Talking Through Next Steps</h3><p>Whatever the results, we’ll explain what they mean in plain language and talk with you about next steps, from retesting to keeping your cat, and the other cats in your home, as healthy as possible.</p>
