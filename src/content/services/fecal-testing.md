faqs:
  - question: "What does a fecal test look for?"
    answer: |-
      <p>A fecal test is a routine test used to find internal parasites. It looks for the eggs that mature parasites living inside the body shed into the stool. Some of these parasites are worms, while others are tiny single-celled organisms called protozoa. The appearance of the eggs under a microscope shows which type of parasite is present.</p><p class="faq-source">Source: <a href="https://vcahospitals.com/know-your-pet/fecal-flotation">VCA Animal Hospitals, Fecal Flotation</a></p>
  - question: "How do I collect a stool sample for my pet’s fecal test?"
    answer: |-
      <p>All that’s needed is about a one-inch piece of fresh stool. Ideally, the sample should be no more than 24 hours old and as free as possible of grass, gravel and kitty litter. Any clean, dry container with a tightly fitting lid, such as a jar or plastic tub, can be used.</p><p class="faq-source">Source: <a href="https://vcahospitals.com/know-your-pet/fecal-flotation">VCA Animal Hospitals, Fecal Flotation</a></p>
  - question: "Why do puppies and kittens need several fecal tests?"
    answer: |-
      <p>Puppies and kittens are frequently infected with intestinal parasites and can be reinfected easily. Very young pets may also carry parasites that are too young to produce eggs, so a single test can miss them. That’s why multiple fecal tests are recommended, and why you should bring a fresh stool sample to each visit in the first series of appointments.</p><p class="faq-source">Source: <a href="https://vcahospitals.com/know-your-pet/fecal-flotation">VCA Animal Hospitals, Fecal Flotation</a></p>
  - question: "Can a fecal test miss parasites?"
    answer: |-
      <p>Yes. A fecal test is a screening test and may not detect every infection. The parasites may be too young to produce eggs, there may be only a few adult parasites, or some parasites may shed eggs in small numbers. If your pet still has symptoms after a negative result, repeat or additional testing may be recommended.</p><p class="faq-source">Source: <a href="https://vcahospitals.com/know-your-pet/fecal-flotation">VCA Animal Hospitals, Fecal Flotation</a></p>
  - question: "Can people catch intestinal parasites from pets?"
    answer: |-
      <p>Some intestinal parasites of dogs and cats can also cause disease in people. Roundworm eggs, for example, stick to many surfaces and mix into soil and dust, so good hygiene is important — especially for children who may be exposed to contaminated pets or areas.</p><p class="faq-source">Source: <a href="https://www.merckvetmanual.com/dog-owners/digestive-disorders-of-dogs/gastrointestinal-parasites-of-dogs">Merck Veterinary Manual, Gastrointestinal Parasites of Dogs</a></p>
