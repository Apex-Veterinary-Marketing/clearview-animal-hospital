faqs:
  - question: "What does a complete blood count (CBC) show?"
    answer: |-
      <p>A CBC looks at your pet’s red blood cells, white blood cells and platelets. Changes can point to anemia, infection, inflammation, dehydration, bleeding risk or bone marrow problems, and they help guide what to test or treat next.</p><p class="faq-source">Source: <a href="https://vcahospitals.com/know-your-pet/complete-blood-count">VCA Animal Hospitals, Complete Blood Count</a></p>
  - question: "Why test my pet’s urine?"
    answer: |-
      <p>A urinalysis checks how concentrated the urine is, its chemistry (such as protein and glucose) and what’s in it under the microscope (cells, crystals and bacteria). It gives information about the kidneys and urinary tract, and it can also point to problems elsewhere in the body, such as diabetes.</p><p class="faq-source">Source: <a href="https://vcahospitals.com/know-your-pet/urinalysis">VCA Animal Hospitals, Urinalysis</a></p>
  - question: "Why should my cat be tested for FIV and FeLV?"
    answer: |-
      <p>Feline immunodeficiency virus (FIV) and feline leukemia virus (FeLV) are serious viral infections. Cornell’s Feline Health Center recommends testing cats when they are first acquired, when they become ill, and again after a possible exposure. FIV spreads mainly through bite wounds.</p><p class="faq-source">Source: <a href="https://www.vet.cornell.edu/departments-centers-and-institutes/cornell-feline-health-center/health-information/feline-health-topics/feline-immunodeficiency-virus">Cornell Feline Health Center, Feline Immunodeficiency Virus</a></p>
  - question: "How often should my dog have a heartworm test?"
    answer: |-
      <p>The American Heartworm Society recommends testing every dog every year, even dogs that take a preventive year-round, because missed or late doses can leave a pet unprotected. Annual testing catches infections early, when they are easier to manage.</p><p class="faq-source">Source: <a href="https://www.heartwormsociety.org/pet-owner-resources/heartworm-basics">American Heartworm Society, Heartworm Basics</a></p>
  - question: "Why do you run bloodwork before anesthesia?"
    answer: |-
      <p>Pre-anesthetic bloodwork checks values like liver enzymes and kidney function to make sure your pet is a good candidate for anesthesia. Abnormal results may lead to fluids or other treatment before the procedure, or to postponing it until the problem is addressed.</p><p class="faq-source">Source: <a href="https://vcahospitals.com/know-your-pet/bloodwork">VCA Animal Hospitals, Preanesthetic Bloodwork</a></p>
