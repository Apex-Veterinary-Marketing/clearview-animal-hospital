      <h2>What to Expect When You Talk With Us</h2><p>End-of-life care begins with a conversation, not a decision. We’ll examine your pet and talk honestly about their condition, what changes to expect, and what can be done to keep them comfortable. Together we’ll look at your pet’s quality of life — appetite, mobility, comfort, and their interest in the things and people they love.</p><p>There’s no rush. You’re welcome to take time to think, talk with your family, and ask us anything, as many times as you need. If you choose euthanasia, we’ll explain each step beforehand so nothing comes as a surprise. You can decide whether you’d like to be with your pet or say goodbye beforehand — both are loving choices.</p><p>Our goal is for your pet’s final moments to be peaceful and free of pain, and for you to feel supported and never hurried.</p>

      <p>There’s rarely one clear moment. If your pet’s quality of life is seriously affected by an illness or injury that can’t be treated, euthanasia may be the kindest choice.   - question: "Can I stay with my pet during euthanasia?"
    answer: |-
      <p>That’s your choice. Some owners want to be present, while others prefer to say goodbye beforehand. It’s a very personal decision, and you should do what feels right for you without pressure from anyone else.</p><p class="faq-source">Source: <a href="https://www.avma.org/resources-tools/pet-owners/petcare/euthanasia">American Veterinary Medical Association, Euthanasia</a></p>
  - question: "How do I talk to my children about it?"
    answer: |-
      <p>Children shouldn’t be left out just because they seem young — being excluded can make grief harder. Give them simple, honest answers. When they’re prepared, children are usually able to accept a pet’s death.</p><p class="faq-source">Source: <a href="https://www.avma.org/resources-tools/pet-owners/petcare/euthanasia">American Veterinary Medical Association, Euthanasia</a></p>
  - question: "Is it normal to grieve this much for a pet?"
    answer: |-
      <p>Yes. Grief after losing a pet can be as real and as deep as grief after losing a person. Leaning on family, friends and pet loss support services can help.</p><p class="faq-source">Source: <a href="https://www.vet.cornell.edu/about-us/outreach/pet-loss-support-hotline">Cornell University College of Veterinary Medicine, Pet Loss Support Hotline</a></p>
