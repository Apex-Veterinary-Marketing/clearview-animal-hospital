      <h2>Help Locate and Reunite Lost Pets</h2><p>Accidents happen, and no pet owner wants to experience the heartache of losing their beloved companion unintentionally. Whether it’s a door left open, a sudden loud noise startling them, or a determined explorer squeezing through a small gap, pets can easily go astray. That’s where microchipping comes into play. It’s a permanent method of identification, implanted under the skin between the shoulder blades of your pet, typically about the size of a grain of rice. If your pet goes missing and is discovered by a shelter or veterinarian, they’ll scan for a microchip. This chip contains a unique number, which, when checked, reveals the owner’s contact information. It’s essential for pet owners to ensure their contact details are accurate and up to date in the microchip database to facilitate a prompt reunion if their pet becomes lost.</p>
  - image: "../../assets/services/microchip-3.jpg"

      <h2>What to Expect When Your Pet Is Microchipped</h2><p>Microchipping is quick and doesn’t require surgery. The chip comes preloaded in a sterile applicator, and it’s placed just under the skin between your pet’s shoulder blades with a quick injection — much like a routine vaccine. Most pets go right back to their normal routine afterward. If your pet is already scheduled for a procedure under anesthesia, such as a spay or neuter, the chip can be placed then instead.</p><p>Once it’s in place, the chip is scanned to make sure it reads correctly, and you’ll receive your pet’s unique microchip number. The last step is the most important one: registering that number with your contact information. A chip that isn’t registered — or that lists an old phone number — can’t lead anyone back to you, so keep your details current whenever you move or change numbers.</p>
  - image: "../../assets/services/microchip-4.jpg"

      <h2>Is Microchipping Right for Your Pet?</h2><p>Any dog or cat can be microchipped, and it’s worth considering for every pet, including indoor cats who might slip out an open door. Microchips are especially helpful for pets who don’t keep a collar on, escape artists, dogs who travel or go to boarding or daycare, and new puppies and kittens getting set up for life.</p><h2>When Should Your Pet Be Microchipped?</h2><p>A microchip only needs to be placed once and is meant to last your pet’s lifetime. Many families choose to do it at a puppy or kitten visit or alongside a spay or neuter, but it can be done at any age. After that, ask us to scan the chip at your pet’s checkups so you know it’s still working, and update your registration any time your contact information changes.</p><h2>Practice Highlights</h2><p>Why Easley families choose Clearview:</p><ul><li><strong>Caring for Easley since 1958</strong> — We’ve been helping pets in Easley and the Upstate for generations.</li><li><strong>Same-day appointments</strong> — Same-day visits are available when you need them.</li><li><strong>Online booking</strong> — Request your pet’s appointment online, any time.</li></ul>

faqs:
  - question: "Does getting a microchip hurt?"
    answer: |-
      <p>The chip is placed with a needle under the skin, and most pets react about as they would to having blood drawn. Anesthesia isn’t required, though some owners choose to have the chip placed while their pet is already under anesthesia for a spay or neuter.</p><p class="faq-source">Source: <a href="https://vcahospitals.com/know-your-pet/microchipping-your-dog">VCA Animal Hospitals, Microchipping Your Dog</a></p>
  - question: "Can a microchip track my pet’s location like a GPS?"
    answer: |-
      <p>No. A microchip has no battery or power source. It simply stores an ID number and only responds when a scanner passes over it, so it can’t show where your pet is. It works when your pet is found and scanned by a shelter or veterinary clinic.</p><p class="faq-source">Source: <a href="https://vcahospitals.com/know-your-pet/microchipping-your-dog">VCA Animal Hospitals, Microchipping Your Dog</a></p>
  - question: "Do I need to register my pet’s microchip?"
    answer: |-
      <p>Yes. The chip only holds a number, so it has to be registered with your contact information to work. Keep your registration up to date whenever you move or change phone numbers — a lost pet may not make it home if the information on file is out of date.</p><p class="faq-source">Source: <a href="https://www.avma.org/resources-tools/pet-owners/petcare/microchips-reunite-pets-families">American Veterinary Medical Association, Microchips reunite pets with families</a></p>
  - question: "If my pet is microchipped, does it still need a collar and ID tags?"
    answer: |-
      <p>Yes. A microchip is permanent, tamper-proof identification, but it doesn’t replace a collar with up-to-date ID tags. Anyone who finds your pet can read a tag right away, while a chip needs a scanner.</p><p class="faq-source">Source: <a href="https://www.avma.org/resources-tools/pet-owners/petcare/microchips-reunite-pets-families">American Veterinary Medical Association, Microchips reunite pets with families</a></p>
  - question: "Who scans lost pets for microchips?"
    answer: |-
      <p>Shelters and humane societies use universal microchip readers and scan the animals that come into their care, and veterinary clinics can scan found pets too. That’s why accurate registration information is so important.</p><p class="faq-source">Source: <a href="https://vcahospitals.com/know-your-pet/microchipping-your-dog">VCA Animal Hospitals, Microchipping Your Dog</a></p>
