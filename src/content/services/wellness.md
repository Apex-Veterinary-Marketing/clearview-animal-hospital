faqs:
  - question: "Why does my dog need a heartworm test if they already take a preventive?"
    answer: |-
      <p>Heartworm preventives are very effective, but they aren’t 100% foolproof. A missed or late dose, or a pill that gets spit out, can leave a pet unprotected. The American Heartworm Society recommends testing every dog every year — even dogs on year-round prevention — so an infection can be caught and treated early.</p><p class="faq-source">Source: <a href="https://www.heartwormsociety.org/pet-owner-resources/heartworm-basics">American Heartworm Society, Heartworm Basics</a></p>
  - question: "My cat stays indoors. Does she still need preventive care?"
    answer: |-
      <p>Yes. Indoor cats still need regular exams, and they can still be exposed to some parasites. Mosquitoes, which spread heartworm, can get into homes, so the American Heartworm Society notes that indoor pets are at risk too. There is no approved treatment for heartworm in cats, which makes prevention especially important.</p><p class="faq-source">Source: <a href="https://www.heartwormsociety.org/pet-owner-resources/heartworm-basics">American Heartworm Society, Heartworm Basics</a></p>
  - question: "Which vaccines does my pet need?"
    answer: |-
      <p>Vaccines fall into two groups. Core vaccines are recommended for all dogs and cats unless there’s a medical reason not to give them. Non-core (lifestyle) vaccines depend on your pet’s risk — for example, whether your dog goes to boarding, daycare or grooming, or spends time where ticks are common. We’ll recommend the vaccines that fit your pet’s lifestyle.</p><p class="faq-source">Source: <a href="https://www.avma.org/resources-tools/pet-owners/petcare/vaccinations">American Veterinary Medical Association, Vaccinating your pet</a></p>
  - question: "Why do you recommend bloodwork when my pet seems healthy?"
    answer: |-
      <p>Pets are good at hiding illness, and some problems show up in the blood before they show up on the outside. A complete blood count, for example, looks at red blood cells, white blood cells and platelets, and changes can point to anemia, infection, inflammation or dehydration. Results from a healthy year also give us a baseline to compare against later.</p><p class="faq-source">Source: <a href="https://vcahospitals.com/know-your-pet/complete-blood-count">VCA Animal Hospitals, Complete Blood Count</a></p>
  - question: "Why do you check my pet’s weight and body condition at every visit?"
    answer: |-
      <p>Nutrition and weight have a big effect on long-term health, so the World Small Animal Veterinary Association recommends a quick nutritional screening for every pet at every visit. Tracking body condition over time helps us spot weight gain or loss early and adjust your pet’s diet before it becomes a problem.</p><p class="faq-source">Source: <a href="https://wsava.org/global-guidelines/global-nutrition-guidelines/">World Small Animal Veterinary Association, Global Nutrition Guidelines</a></p>
