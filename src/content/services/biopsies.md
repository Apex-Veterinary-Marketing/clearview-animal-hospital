      <h2>Pet Biopsies in Easley, SC</h2><p>Welcome to Clearview Animal Hospital, where our veterinary team, led by Dr. Aimee Seguin, helps Easley families get real answers when a lump or patch of skin needs a closer look.</p><h2>Understanding Pet Biopsies</h2><p>Finding a new lump on your dog or cat, or a patch of skin that just won’t heal, can be worrying. Many lumps are harmless, but some are not, and there’s no reliable way to tell them apart just by looking or feeling. A biopsy gives you a real answer.</p><p>A biopsy is a procedure in which a small piece of tissue, or sometimes an entire lump, is removed so it can be examined under a microscope. At Clearview Animal Hospital, biopsies are one of the <a href="/services/surgical">surgical services</a> we offer. Samples are sent to a veterinary pathologist, a veterinarian who specializes in diagnosing disease from tissue. The results tell us what the problem is, such as an infection, an allergic or immune-related skin condition, or a benign or cancerous growth, so we can recommend the right treatment and help you understand what to expect.</p>

      <h2>What to Expect During a Biopsy</h2><p>From the first exam to the pathologist’s report, here’s how a biopsy works at Clearview.</p><h3>Exam and First Steps</h3><p>We’ll look at the lump or affected area, ask how long it has been there and whether it has changed, and check your pet’s overall health. In some cases, a fine needle aspirate, collecting a few cells with a small needle, is a helpful first step, and we’ll let you know if a biopsy is the better choice.</p><h3>Keeping Your Pet Comfortable</h3><p>How the biopsy is done depends on where the area is and on your pet. Many skin biopsies only need a local anesthetic to numb the spot, while other pets need sedation or general anesthesia to stay comfortable and still. If anesthesia is recommended, pre-anesthetic bloodwork from our in-house laboratory helps confirm your pet is ready.</p><h3>Collecting the Sample</h3><p>We remove either a small section of tissue or the entire mass, and most small skin biopsy sites are closed with just a few stitches. The sample is then sent to a veterinary pathologist for examination.</p><h3>Aftercare and Results</h3><p>We’ll explain how to care for the biopsy site at home, including keeping your pet from licking or scratching it. When the pathologist’s report comes back, we’ll go over the results with you and talk through the next steps.</p>
