  - question: "Can I give my pet human pain medicine?"
    answer: |-
      <p>Not without talking to your veterinarian first. Some common pain relievers for people can be dangerous for pets — acetaminophen is especially dangerous for cats, and NSAIDs like ibuprofen shouldn’t be given unless a veterinarian approves them.</p><p class="faq-source">Source: <a href="https://www.fda.gov/animal-veterinary/animal-health-literacy/get-facts-about-pain-relievers-pets">U.S. Food and Drug Administration, Get the Facts about Pain Relievers for Pets</a></p>
  - question: "Why does my pet need an exam before a prescription can be refilled?"
    answer: |-
      <p>Veterinarians can only prescribe within a veterinarian-client-patient relationship (VCPR), and pets on ongoing medication need to be rechecked regularly to make sure the dose is still effective and safe. How often depends on your pet’s health and the medication.</p><p class="faq-source">Source: <a href="https://www.avma.org/resources-tools/animal-health-and-welfare/animal-health/pharmacy/prescriptions-and-pharmacies-faq-pet-owner">American Veterinary Medical Association, Prescriptions and pharmacies: FAQs for pet owners</a></p>
  - question: "Can I fill my pet’s prescription at another pharmacy?"
    answer: |-
      <p>In most cases, yes. Most states require veterinarians to provide a prescription on request when a medication is medically necessary, so you can choose where to fill it. We can also provide a prescription to a local pharmacy that carries the medication, either by writing it or calling it in.</p><p class="faq-source">Source: <a href="https://www.avma.org/resources-tools/animal-health-and-welfare/animal-health/pharmacy/prescriptions-and-pharmacies-faq-pet-owner">American Veterinary Medical Association, Prescriptions and pharmacies: FAQs for pet owners</a></p>
  - question: "Any tips for giving my cat a pill?"
    answer: |-
      <p>Try hiding the pill in a small amount of wet food, tuna or a soft treat when your cat is hungry. If you need to give it directly, place it as far back on the tongue as you can, close the mouth, gently stroke the throat, then follow with a little water from a syringe. If pilling is a struggle, ask about having the medication made into a flavored liquid or treat.</p><p class="faq-source">Source: <a href="https://vcahospitals.com/know-your-pet/giving-pills-to-cats">VCA Animal Hospitals, Giving Pills to Cats</a></p>
