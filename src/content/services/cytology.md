      <h2>Pet Cytology in Easley, SC</h2><p>Welcome to Clearview Animal Hospital, where our veterinary team uses cytology to help find answers for pets in Easley, Pickens, Clemson, Liberty and Greenville.</p><h2>Understanding Cytology</h2><p>When your dog or cat has a new lump, a patch of irritated skin or an ear that keeps bothering them, the first question is always the same: what is it? Cytology is often one of the quickest ways to start finding out.</p><p>Cytology is the examination of cells under a microscope. A small sample of cells is collected, from a lump, the skin, the ears or a body fluid, and studied for clues such as bacteria, yeast, inflammation or abnormal cells. At Clearview Animal Hospital, cytology is one of the diagnostic tests offered through our <a href="/services/laboratory">in-house laboratory</a> services. Depending on the sample, it may be examined here or sent to a veterinary pathologist for review.</p>

      <h2>What to Expect During Your Pet’s Cytology</h2><p>Cytology is a simple test that most pets tolerate well. Here’s how it works.</p><h3>An Exam to Choose the Sample</h3><p>It starts with an exam, so we can decide where and how to collect the sample.</p><h3>Collecting the Cells</h3><p>For a lump, the most common method is a fine needle aspirate: a very small needle is placed into the lump to collect a few cells. For skin or ears, we may gently swab the area, press a glass slide against a moist sore or lightly scrape the skin’s surface. Most pets tolerate this well, and many samples can be collected without sedation.</p><h3>Looking Under the Microscope</h3><p>The cells are placed on a slide, stained and examined under a microscope. Cytology can often tell whether a problem is caused by inflammation or infection, and whether a lump looks like a benign growth or something more concerning.</p><h3>Results and Next Steps</h3><p>Cytology looks at individual cells, while a <a href="/services/biopsies">biopsy</a> removes a piece of tissue so a pathologist can see how the cells are arranged. If the cytology results aren’t conclusive, or a lump needs a more definite answer, we may recommend a biopsy as the next step. Either way, we’ll go over the results with you and explain what we recommend.</p>
