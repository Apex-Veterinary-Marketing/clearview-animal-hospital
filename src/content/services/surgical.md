      <h2>What to Expect During Your Pet’s Surgery</h2><p>Surgery starts well before the procedure and continues after your pet goes home. Here’s how we care for your pet at each stage.</p><h3>Pre-Operative Evaluation</h3><p>A pre-operative evaluation, an exam and, in most cases, bloodwork from our in-house laboratory, helps us make sure your pet is ready for anesthesia and lets us tailor the plan to your pet. We’ll explain the procedure, answer your questions and give you instructions for the night before, such as when to stop feeding.</p><h3>Anesthesia and Monitoring</h3><p>On the day of surgery, your pet is placed under general anesthesia so they feel no pain during the procedure. Your pet is closely watched through surgery and while waking up.</p><h3>The Procedure</h3><p>Our team performs the surgery with careful attention to your pet’s safety and comfort. Orthopedic procedures are performed by a traveling orthopedic surgeon, so your pet can be treated close to home.</p><h3>Discharge and Recovery at Home</h3><p>We’ll go over discharge instructions with you when it’s time to go home. Recovery at home matters just as much: you’ll learn how to keep the incision clean and dry, how to limit activity, how to give any medications, and what to watch for. If you have questions after you get home, we’re a phone call away.</p>

      <h2>Why Surgery is Important for Your Pet</h2><p>Many surgeries are planned, like spaying or neutering a young pet or removing a lump so it can be tested. Others are recommended after an exam or diagnostic test finds a problem. Either way, the right procedure at the right time can protect your pet’s health for years to come.</p><h3><strong>Prevention</strong></h3><p>Spaying females can prevent uterine infections and reduce the risk of breast cancer. Neutering males eliminates the risk of testicular cancer, lowers the risk of an enlarged prostate, and reduces roaming and urine marking. Both procedures also prevent unplanned litters.</p><h3><strong>Answers</strong></h3><p>When a growth needs a closer look, a <a href="/services/biopsies">biopsy</a> or <a href="/services/tumor-removal">tumor removal</a> lets the tissue be examined so you know exactly what it was and what comes next.</p><h3><strong>Comfort and Quality of Life</strong></h3><p>Surgery can relieve pain, remove a problem that medication alone can’t fix, and help your pet get back to eating, playing and enjoying life with your family.</p><h2>Signs Your Pet May Need Surgery</h2><p>Talk with us if you notice any of the following:</p><ul><li>A new or growing lump</li><li>A wound that won’t heal</li><li>Your pet hasn’t been spayed or neutered yet</li><li>Limping or trouble using a leg</li><li>Ongoing vomiting, a swollen belly or signs of abdominal pain</li></ul><p>If something seems off, a quick exam can tell us whether surgery makes sense for your pet.</p><h2>How Often Should Your Pet Have Surgery?</h2><p>Most surgeries are one-time procedures, and timing depends on the reason. Spay and neuter timing varies with your pet’s species, breed, size and health, so we’ll recommend the right age for your pet. For lumps and other concerns, sooner is usually better, and a quick exam can tell us whether surgery makes sense.</p><h2>Practice Highlights</h2><p>Why families choose Clearview for their pet’s surgery:</p><ul><li><strong>In-house laboratory</strong>: pre-surgical bloodwork can be run right here at our hospital.</li><li><strong>Traveling orthopedic surgeon</strong>: orthopedic procedures are performed by a traveling orthopedic surgeon, so your pet can be treated close to home.</li><li><strong>Digital radiography</strong>: X-rays help with planning and follow-up when they’re needed.</li><li><strong>Family-focused care</strong>: you and your pet are part of the Clearview family, and we’ve cared for Easley pets since 1958.</li><li><strong>Payment options</strong>: CareCredit and Cherry can help you plan for the cost of surgery.</li></ul>
