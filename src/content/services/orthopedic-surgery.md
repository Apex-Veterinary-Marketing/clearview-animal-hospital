      <h2>Orthopedic Care for Pets Near Easley, SC</h2><p>Bones, joints and ligaments carry your pet through every walk, zoomie and jump onto the couch.   - question: "What is a cruciate ligament tear in dogs?"
    answer: |-
      <p>The cranial cruciate ligament (CCL) stabilizes the knee, much like the ACL in people. In dogs it usually weakens gradually rather than tearing from one sudden injury, and obesity, fitness, genetics and breed all play a role. Signs include hind-leg lameness, trouble rising from a sit, and reluctance to jump. Surgery is generally the best option because it’s the only way to permanently control the knee’s instability.</p><p class="faq-source">Source: <a href="https://www.acvs.org/small-animal/cranial-cruciate-ligament-disease">American College of Veterinary Surgeons, Cranial Cruciate Ligament Disease</a></p>
  - question: "My small dog skips on a back leg now and then. What could it be?"
    answer: |-
      <p>An occasional skip can be a sign of a luxating patella — a kneecap that slides out of its groove. It’s most common in small breeds such as Yorkshire terriers, Chihuahuas, Pomeranians and miniature poodles. Mild cases may simply be monitored, while surgery is most often considered for more advanced grades.</p><p class="faq-source">Source: <a href="https://www.acvs.org/small-animal/patellar-luxations">American College of Veterinary Surgeons, Patellar Luxation</a></p>
  - question: "How long is recovery after orthopedic surgery?"
    answer: |-
      <p>Most pets need activity restricted for several weeks after knee surgery, with follow-up exams and X-rays to check healing. Rehabilitation started soon after surgery can speed recovery and improve results. Once healed, many dogs return to an active lifestyle.</p><p class="faq-source">Source: <a href="https://www.acvs.org/small-animal/cranial-cruciate-ligament-disease">American College of Veterinary Surgeons, Cranial Cruciate Ligament Disease</a></p>
  - question: "How is a knee injury diagnosed?"
    answer: |-
      <p>A veterinarian can often diagnose a cruciate ligament tear with a hands-on exam that checks for abnormal movement in the knee. Mild sedation may help relax the muscles, especially with partial tears, and X-rays are used to evaluate the joint and plan surgery.</p><p class="faq-source">Source: <a href="https://www.acvs.org/small-animal/cranial-cruciate-ligament-disease">American College of Veterinary Surgeons, Cranial Cruciate Ligament Disease</a></p>
