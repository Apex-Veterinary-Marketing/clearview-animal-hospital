      <h2>Pet Urinalysis in Easley, SC</h2><p>Welcome to Clearview Animal Hospital, where our veterinary team uses urinalysis to help pets in Easley, Pickens, Clemson, Liberty and Greenville feel their best.</p><h2>Understanding Urinalysis</h2><p>Accidents in the house, straining in the litter box, drinking more water than usual: changes in your pet’s bathroom habits are worth paying attention to. A urine test is one of the simplest ways to find out what’s behind them.</p><p>A urinalysis looks at your pet’s urine in several ways: its color and clarity, how concentrated it is, its chemistry (such as pH, protein and glucose) and what’s in it under the microscope, like cells, crystals and bacteria. At Clearview Animal Hospital, urinalysis is one of the tests handled by our <a href="/services/laboratory">in-house laboratory</a>. The results tell us how well the kidneys are working and whether there are signs of infection, inflammation, bladder stones or crystals, and they can also point to problems elsewhere in the body, such as diabetes or liver disease.</p>

      <h2>What to Expect During Your Pet’s Urinalysis</h2><p>A urinalysis is a gentle, straightforward test. Here’s what the process looks like.</p><h3>An Exam and Your Observations</h3><p>First, we’ll examine your pet and ask about any changes you’ve noticed, such as how often your pet is urinating, how much they’re drinking, and whether you’ve seen blood or straining.</p><h3>Collecting the Right Sample</h3><p>Sometimes a sample caught while your pet urinates normally is all we need, and we may ask you to bring one from home. For other tests, especially when we’re checking for a bladder infection, a cleaner sample collected directly from the bladder with a small needle (cystocentesis) gives more reliable results. It’s quick, and most pets tolerate it well.</p><h3>A Fresh Sample for Accurate Results</h3><p>Because urine changes as it sits, a fresh sample gives the most accurate picture, so we’ll let you know how and when to collect it. The sample is then tested in our in-house laboratory.</p><h3>Reviewing Results Together</h3><p>Once the results are in, we’ll explain what they mean, what we recommend next and whether any follow-up testing, such as bloodwork or a urine culture, would help. Ultrasound and digital radiography can also help us look for bladder stones and other problems a urine test can’t show on its own.</p>

      <h2>Why Urinalysis is Important for Your Pet</h2><p>Urine holds a lot of information about your pet’s health. A urinalysis is a simple test that can reveal problems in the urinary tract and beyond.</p><h3><strong>Insight Into Kidney Health</strong></h3><p>How concentrated your pet’s urine is, and whether it contains protein, helps show how well the kidneys are working. This is especially valuable for senior pets and pets with kidney disease.</p><h3><strong>Finding Infection, Crystals and Stones</strong></h3><p>Looking at urine under the microscope can reveal bacteria, inflammation and crystals that may point to infection or bladder stones, so we can start the right treatment.</p><h3><strong>Clues to Conditions Elsewhere in the Body</strong></h3><p>Glucose and other changes in the urine can point to diabetes, liver disease or other conditions, often guiding which tests to run next.</p><h2>Signs Your Pet May Need a Urinalysis</h2><p>Urinalysis is a valuable part of wellness testing, especially for senior pets and pets with kidney disease, diabetes or other ongoing conditions. We may also recommend one if your pet is:</p><ul><li>Urinating more or less often than usual</li><li>Straining or crying out while urinating</li><li>Having accidents or urinating outside the litter box</li><li>Licking the genital area often</li><li>Drinking much more water than normal, or passing blood in the urine</li></ul><p>A male cat who strains in the litter box and passes little or no urine may have a urethral blockage, which is an emergency. Call us or seek care right away.</p><h2>How Often Should Your Pet Have a Urinalysis?</h2><p>For many pets, a urinalysis fits naturally into the yearly wellness visit alongside bloodwork. Senior pets and pets with kidney disease, diabetes or a history of urinary problems often need it more often so we can track changes over time. Any time your pet’s bathroom habits change, a urinalysis is a good place to start.</p><h2>Practice Highlights</h2><p>Why families trust Clearview when their pet has urinary concerns:</p><ul><li><strong>In-house laboratory:</strong> urine and blood tests can be run right here at our hospital.</li><li><strong>Ultrasound and digital radiography:</strong> imaging helps us look for bladder stones and other problems a urine test can’t show on its own.</li><li><strong>Same-day appointments:</strong> when your pet isn’t feeling well, same-day visits are available.</li><li><strong>Caring for Easley since 1958:</strong> a trusted local animal hospital for generations of Upstate pets.</li><li><strong>Payment options:</strong> Cherry and CareCredit payment plans can help you plan for the cost of care.</li></ul>
